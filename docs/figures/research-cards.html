Research Notes as Poster Cards

Natural-background copy sheet. Wording stays close to .docs/research.md, with light polish for poster placement.

Research question

What is the story told among these human stories?

Can we find a familiar or fundamental human story across modern books about humans and big history?

  • To make sense of many stories: is there a kernel of truth to piece together?
  • There is not time to read or study all of them closely.
  • The books are modern stories in the sub-discipline of humans and big history, some of which may be incomplete or untrue.
  • The question is whether something stands out as making us human, or making the story human.
Comparison

Common or different?

From the outset, the motivation was comparison. The question may first look like commonality, but finding what is common necessitates comparison.

  • Is there some idea that emerges for all human stories relative to a cultural baseline?
  • This lends itself to a tool that discovers change between two stories.
Method

Why allotax?

From a big-picture view, allotax makes it immediate which words are comparatively important to each story.

  • It can be seen readily with allotax.
  • It quantifies what is important to each story, resting on mathematical foundations: RTD, Zipf, and rank.
  • The method tells us where to compute, how the comparison is derived, and can output tables or matrices.
  • There was no advantage here to inventing a new Zipf comparison method from scratch.
Not this

Not sentiment, not timeline.

We were not looking for an overall measurement of sentiment or temporal narrative time.

  • Importance in size, not sentiment.
  • Types and ranks, not feelings.
  • No causality claim.
Quantifiable argument

Measured comparison, not selected examples.

  • Each word or phrase has a rank in each system.
  • Each word or phrase has an RTD contribution toward the difference between systems.
  • The diamond, contour lines, and word list are different views of the same measured comparison.
  • Tables and matrices let the argument be checked across books, not just by selected examples.
Weaknesses

Limitations.

  • No narrative or temporal instrument.
  • No causality.
  • Wikipedia featured articles are a popular-cultural baseline, not culture itself.
  • Some books are anthropology more than big history.
Data

Books plus WikiText baseline.

  • Use an existing mostly sanitized dataset for WikiText.
  • WikiText-2 is about 2M training tokens and roughly 600 articles; WikiText-103 is roughly 50x larger in training data.
  • Google Books has scale, but scale is not a cultural baseline by itself.
  • A usable baseline would need to distinguish popular-cultural language from scientific/reference components.
  • The featured-list filter keeps closer to cultural popularity, while obscure science/literature pages are partly filtered out.
  • Books were selected for human history or anthropology, cultural visibility, and chronology.
Data note

Why not Google Books?

Google Books is attractive because of scale, but it mixes popular books with scientific and reference language. Before it can serve as a cultural baseline, those components need to be separated. WikiText is imperfect, but more tractable for this pass.

Why to how

Why to how

It may seem impossible to pull a story out without narrative time, but it could be like pulling the logline out of a plot.

  • What stayed core to the stories?
  • What became turbulent?
  • Example: we, our, humans are consistent; what turbulence gathers around them?
  • The characters of the story may say more about what the story is than how the author ordered the plot.
Contrast

Stories define themselves by contrast.

Philosophically, humans tend to define their stories by contrast.

  • A neutral ground may not display much.
  • Where stories decide to differ may reveal where the important story is.
Why rank?

Each system has an ordering.

In systems, there is an ordering of what is most important: who are the rich getting richer?

  • Not just whether a word was frequent.
  • Instead: how high is that word relative to another system?
  • Compare against a cultural baseline or against another system of the same kind.
How

How method answers

  • Back to the why we answered.
  • Visual spacing and contour lines guide the eye to words most important to one story with respect to another.
  • RTD contribution lists show what is important toward each system and how important it is.
  • The method answers with a measured claim: not "these words felt important," but "these words repeatedly contributed most to the measured difference between systems."
  • Repeated high-RTD words and phrases across comparisons become evidence for stable story-elements.
  • rank1 -> rank2 plus RTD contribution makes both direction and size of difference visible.
Book v Book

What makes a book distinct?

  • Across a book-v-book slice, bring forward the most important language from each story.
  • The difference shows what makes a human story other than simply saying "human."
  • See where the books decide to differ.
  • Repeated resurfacing matters.
  • Down the column: LHS RTD to RHS. Across the row: RHS RTD.
Wiki v Book

What repeats against background?

  • Compare each story against the same cultural background.
  • If the stories have commonality, it should show here.
  • What repeats as important across human stories?
Poster claim draft

From many human stories to measurable contrast.

The poster does not claim to recover one timeless human story. It asks which words and phrases repeatedly become important when modern human-history books are compared against WikiText and against each other. The evidence is the repeated rank-turbulent vocabulary: the measured language each story uses to make "human" distinctive.
